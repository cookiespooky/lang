# Baseline — 2026-08-16

## Context

Target language: French.

Goal: recover and develop conversational French for natural communication with French-speaking acquaintances.

Prior exposure: approximately one year of French as a secondary/non-major language at a linguistics faculty, around 15 years ago. Much of the knowledge is expected to be dormant.

Other language resources: fluent English, usable German, linguistics background.

## Available production evidence

Spontaneous samples produced before formal diagnostic work:

> Salut! Je m’appelle Antoine. Je habite a Russie. Je suis un programmiste et linguiste.

> Je parle anglais et allemand. J’invente web services

## What is already accessible

- basic greeting/self-introduction;
- `je` + common present-tense verb structures;
- `Je m’appelle`;
- `je suis`;
- `je parle`;
- basic language/profession-related vocabulary;
- independent construction of short declarative clauses.

## Friction visible at baseline

- elision is not automatic (`Je habite`);
- country/location prepositions need reconstruction (`a Russie`);
- accents/orthography are not reliably active;
- lexical choice shows transfer/non-idiomatic construction (`programmiste`, `web services`, likely unintended use of `inventer`);
- broader tense system, comprehension, pronunciation and conversational interaction remain untested.

## Assessment confidence

Low-to-moderate. The sample is very small and should not be converted into a precise CEFR label. The strongest current conclusion is qualitative: this is reactivation of dormant French, not first exposure to French.

## Next comparison point

After initial conversational diagnostic work, compare against this note for:

- length and complexity of spontaneous turns;
- ability to retrieve high-frequency verbs/forms without prompting;
- accuracy of articles/prepositions/elision;
- reduction in English lexical transfer;
- ability to narrate past events and express plans;
- comprehension of French-only prompts;
- degree of prompting required.
