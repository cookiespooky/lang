# Teaching strategy

Status: active — provisional
Last updated: 2026-08-16

## Prefer

- Retrieval before explanation: first test whether old French knowledge can be recovered.
- Conversation and production as the main diagnostic instrument.
- Short, targeted explanations after an observed need rather than generic grammar lectures.
- Natural French chunks and reformulation when lexical transfer produces understandable but non-idiomatic French.
- Strategic comparison with English or German when it makes a distinction immediately clearer.
- Increasing use of French as comprehension allows.
- Selective correction focused on patterns and high-value conversational errors.

## Avoid

- Treating the learner as a complete beginner solely because active French is rusty.
- Following a generic textbook sequence without evidence.
- Correcting every orthographic detail while the learner is trying to regain conversational flow.
- Mistaking linguistic understanding for spontaneous speaking ability.

## Evidence

- Learner studied French previously but has had roughly 15 years of attrition.
- Learner independently recovered several basic French structures in spontaneous writing.
- Learner has a linguistics background and strong English plus usable German, making concise contrastive explanation potentially efficient.

## Hypotheses to test

- Dormant grammar/vocabulary may reactivate quickly under contextual prompting.
- Production, not conceptual understanding, may be the principal bottleneck.
- Explicit linguistic terminology may accelerate some explanations, but should not displace actual French use.

These are hypotheses, not established preferences. Update this file as teaching evidence accumulates.
